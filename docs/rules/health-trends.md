## At a glance

- A medicine's label is never cut at its END to fit a narrow column: the dosage form gives way first, then the end of the brand; the strength is the last thing cut (`nameLines`). (+1 more)
- A rule for ONE name never promises that two names come out different: the names of one chart are laid out TOGETHER (`namesApart`); a "never" property is run over pairs BUILT to be mistaken for each other.
- One step of a fix never undoes another (`namesApart`): a step is taken only when it leaves FEWER pairs alike on the whole chart; a case that cannot be told apart is a failing test (`DEFECT-HM-NAMES`).
- A number is never cut, or parted from its unit, to make it fit; a decimal point is never parted from its number; and a rule about medicine labels is tried on labels as real sheets write them (`20 mg+50 mg`).
- "Every" is never said of what a sample has shown, nor "the labels differ" taken for "the labels tell them apart": a shape that matters is COUNTED, whole, over fixed pools; a test's name says what the test tries.
- A strength is never cut without looking at the other strengths of that medicine on the chart (`layOut`, `medicineOf`); the strength takes the line only where the medicine's name is shown no less than before.
- No language feature new to the doctor app (`\p{…}` classes, `matchAll`) goes into the page every doctor opens without asking what a browser that lacks it will do: there it fails to load the page.
- Part of a number is never plotted: what stands on either side of a comma with a digit directly on each side of it is not plotted (`client/src/lib/numericInvSeries.ts`).
- A drawing that carries numbers is never shrunk to fit a narrow screen: the health-trend chart keeps its size and scrolls sideways; its names stay put.
- HM duration overrides `{ [name]: { sf, upto } }`: a blank side means use the derived date, not hide; an override equal to the derived range is dropped; orphaned keys ignored, never purged.
- How it works — `HealthMonitoringView.tsx` is a thin shell (ranges, override state/mutations); `HealthTrendsChart.tsx` renders and edits — one chart, one place to edit; the old Drug timeline and Export card are gone.
- Health chart window ≠ filter: the time dropdown clamps only the LOWER bound; never make a running medication disappear from a narrow window (the bar clips with a chevron).
- Health chart: apply the override first, then the window, so a bar the doctor just moved into view is not hidden.
- Health chart: nothing vanishes quietly — a ticked parameter with no in-window reading keeps a lane saying so; unreadable dates are dropped from the plot but counted in an amber note.
- Health monitoring editing is owner-only (`canEdit`, enforced server-side too); log from the mutation's `onSuccess`; the patient id travels in the mutation payload, not a closure.
- Health chart bars run from earliest mention to latest mention or the Drug-history End (`lib/drugCourse.ts`); unreadable durations stay understated — don't guess in `drugHistorySummary.ts` (+1 more)
- Health chart edits: a typo is not an instruction — reject an unreadable date, one >5 years out, or an end before the start, in place, storing nothing; `buildTrack`'s clamp is only a backstop.
- Health chart keyboard: Enter commits, Escape abandons; Escape must set a ref BEFORE blurring, because `blur()` runs the commit synchronously.
- ⚕️ `numericInvSeries.ts`: a comma with a digit directly on each side cannot be read — neither side is plotted; comma + space is punctuation. Defects kept: spaced/grouped numbers, signs (+3 more)
- Health chart readings carry their parsed `ms` from the memo that builds the series; don't move date parsing back into render.
- ⚕️ Narrow health chart is never shrunk (scrolls; names in a second `viewBox`); `timelineLabel.ts` never cuts a number or parts it from its unit, and keeps strengths apart (+10 more)
- Health chart month ticks (`ticksInPlot`): only months INSIDE the plot get a line; names keep one rhythm (every 1st/2nd/3rd, never a mix); the month under way at the left edge is named, no line.
- Known limit — Health chart on a touch screen: a point's exact date is its `<title>`, which a finger cannot hover; month labels and the lists under the chart are what a phone has.
- How it works — `hmSelectedDrugs` has no UI writer but is still seeded on patient load and carried by the family-link flow, so the column and its data stay.

# The rules, in full

## Patterns We Do Not Use

_From the root `CLAUDE.md`._

- We do not cut a medicine's label at its END to fit a narrow column. A label starts with its dosage form: `Tablet. Entaliv 0.5 mg` and `Tablet. Entaliv 1 mg` both became `Tablet. Enta…` on a phone's health-trend chart — two bars under one name. The dosage form gives way first, then the end of the brand; the strength is the last thing cut (`nameLines` in `client/src/lib/timelineLabel.ts`). And "the dosage form" is not whatever stands before the first full stop — `Pot.` and `Sod.` are part of a name, and left off they made potassium and sodium chloride one; `IV Infusion.` is a form the app does not know by name, and treated as part of the name it took the whole line from the brand. Three reviews found three such cases; a property over many names found two more that examples had not.
- We do not promise, from a rule for ONE name, that two names will come out different. Two lines of thirteen letters do not hold every label, and whatever a rule leaves off, some pair differs in exactly that: with the rule above in place a tablet and an injection of one medicine and strength were one name on a phone's chart, two salts of calcium another, and four parameters of Hb electrophoresis four lanes all called `Hb / Electrophores…` — while the code and three documents said "never" (fourth review, before release). The names of one chart are laid out TOGETHER (`namesApart`): any that come out the same are written again so that what they differ in is what is shown. And a property that says "never" is run over pairs BUILT to be mistaken for each other — the one that passed had left out every pair of brands sharing three letters.
- We do not let one step of a fix undo another. The first `namesApart` took whichever of two ways applied, round after round: of four strengths of one tablet it told one pair apart by showing their last part, which made them the same as the other pair, and the next round turned it back — two strengths under one name again, and `12.5 mg` on a line by itself as if it were the whole strength of a 5 + 160 + 12.5 tablet (fifth review, before release). A step is taken only when it leaves FEWER pairs alike on the whole chart than before; what is brought into view keeps a mark beside it; and each case found that cannot be told apart in two short lines is written down as a failing test (`DEFECT-HM-NAMES`), not left for a reader to find. And we say of a rule only what it holds: "fewer pairs alike" ends the work and stops two rounds turning each other back — it does not mean a pair once told apart is never put together again, which the code's own comment, a test's name and two documents claimed for a review round.
- We do not cut a number, or part it from its unit, to make it fit — and we do not try a rule about medicine labels only on labels we made up. On the phone chart's names a cut fell wherever the line ran out: `12.5 mg + 500 mg` was shown `12.5 mg + 50…`. And the word a strength parts at was looked for between spaces, while a combination picked from the medicines table has none round its plus (the one label in the tests that came from a real sheet is written so, `Edeloss 20 mg+50 mg`; the table itself was not read for this): `10 mg+160 mg+25 mg` was shown `10…mg+25 mg` — a tablet of 10 mg + 25 mg, to the eye; the unit beside the 10 was the hidden 160's (sixth review, before release). The reviews before it had passed it by, because every example in the code, its tests and three documents was written with spaces: nobody had looked at how a real label writes one. An AMOUNT — a number with the short word after it — is not cut into where a line can hold it, and one too long for that keeps its NUMBER whole (the rule's first form did not look for such an amount at all, and `1,200,000 IU` was cut to `1…`: seventh review); a decimal point is never parted from its number (`.5 mg` shown as `5 mg` is ten times the dose); and the rule is held by a property over labels written both ways (`client/src/lib/timelineLabel.ts`). (On a WIDE screen a name is still one line cut at twenty characters wherever that falls, as in production — `Tablet. Metformin 5…` for 500 mg; closing it takes the two-line names there, which changes the chart the physician knows: `FUNCTIONAL-AUDIT.md`.)
- We do not say "every" of what a sample has shown, and we do not take "the labels differ" for "the labels tell them apart". A property that drew a few thousand random charts a run said every pair on a chart of confusable medicines was told apart, and passed through two reviews — while one chart in a hundred of a single shape, a medicine in two forms and two strengths, was not four names; and where the names did differ, a dose change could read `10 mg+160 mg+…` on both bars (seventh review, before release). A shape that matters is COUNTED, whole, over fixed pools (1,210,776 charts in `timelineLabel.test.ts`), and what is counted is what a doctor reads off the label: two strengths of one medicine never show the same numbers. And the pools are part of the claim: "one of them written another way" was first counted over four ways of writing a form, all of them words the app knows — over the ten forms of the test beside it, 288 charts in half a million still read `10 mg+160 mg+…` for 12.5 and for 25 (eighth review). A test's name says what the test tries.
- We do not cut a strength without looking at the other strengths of that medicine on the chart. Laid out one at a time, two labels that differed in something else — the form typed `Tablet` with no full stop, or another form altogether — each cut a long strength at its end, and a dose change read `10 mg+160 mg+…` on both bars; the rule that compares strengths ran only for names that had come out identical (eighth review, fixed after the release it reviewed). Where another strength of a medicine is on the chart, its strength has the second line to itself and is cut by what it has to be told from (`layOut`, `medicineOf` in `client/src/lib/timelineLabel.ts`). And a fix for one thing a label says is checked against the other things it says: that line was taken from the END of the brand, and for one round on `dev` `Tablet Exforg…` stood over a strength with `Exforge` on the same chart — the dose told apart, the medicine no longer (ninth review, before release). A guard written for "what the two medicines share" then missed the shorter of the two names, and a name behind a form the app cannot set aside (tenth review). The rule that stands needs no judgement, and so it can be counted: the strength takes the line only where the new lines show as much of the medicine's name as the name shows by itself — counted against the release, not one label in a million and a half shows less.

- We do not bring a language feature into the page every doctor opens without asking what a browser that lacks it will do. The amount rule was first written with `\p{…}` classes and `matchAll`, neither used anywhere else in the doctor app — and a browser without the first (Firefox before 78) does not show a wrong label, it fails to load the page. It is written with plain classes and `exec`, and a test reads the file for both (seventh review, before release).

- We do not plot part of a number. The parts of a stored result are joined by commas — a comma and nothing else, no space — so a comma with a digit directly on each side of it cannot be read: `WBC:1,200` is 1,200, or 1.2, or a 1 and another box's 200. What stands on either side of such a comma is not plotted (`client/src/lib/numericInvSeries.ts`); a comma followed by a space is punctuation, and what a typed comma MEANS is the physician's to say — which leaves a NUMBER typed with a comma and a space inside it (`1, 200/cmm`) read as its first part, as production reads it, and that is written down as a failing test (`DEFECT-HM-SPACED`) rather than guessed at. It took three reviews, each finding a wrong number already in production: `1,200` drawn as 1; then `1,200/cmm`, because the guard knew one shape of what follows; then a ferritin typed `>2,000` drawn as 0, because nobody had looked at the comma from its OTHER side. A guard is written for the thing itself, not for the one case that was reported — and tested over every box of the catalog.

- We do not shrink a drawing that carries numbers to fit a narrow screen. The health-trend chart was drawn at half size on a phone and a lab value stood 5px tall — a number a doctor cannot read is one they will guess. It keeps its size and scrolls sideways; its names stay put as a second window on the same drawing.

## Storage string protocols (the data model hiding in strings)

_From `client/CLAUDE.md`._

- **HM duration overrides:** `Patient.hmDrugDates` / `Patient.hmSymptomDates` are both `{ [name]: { sf, upto } }` (`DrugDateMap` in `lib/hmDates.ts`), values parsed by `cellToDate`. Keys are the exact drug name from `drugMentionRanges()` / the exact trimmed complaint text from `symptomMentionRanges()`. A blank or unparseable side means "use the derived date" — it does **not** hide the bar (this differs from the pre-2026-07 `hmDrugDates` semantics, when a blank `sf` meant "don't draw"). New values are written as `dd/mm/yyyy`; older `"26 Jul 2026"` labels from the removed panel still parse and are rewritten on the next edit of that field. A key whose range resolves to exactly the derived one is **dropped, not stored** — an override that changes nothing would still render dashed, carry a ✎ and write an audit line, all of which mean "moved away from the record". Orphaned keys (renamed drug, edited complaint text) are ignored, never purged.

## Health monitoring tab (idsp)

_From `client/CLAUDE.md`._

`HealthMonitoringView.tsx` is now a thin shell: it derives the ranges and owns the override state/mutations, and `HealthTrendsChart.tsx` does the rendering plus editing. The old "Drug timeline" SVG and its per-drug SF/Upto panel were removed (2026-07) — one chart, one place to edit. The decorative "Export Patient's Data" card went with them: a button that looks actionable and does nothing is worse than no button on a clinical screen.

- **Window ≠ filter.** The time dropdown (3m / 6m / 1y / all, default **all**, not persisted) clamps only the LOWER bound. A track whose effective end is inside the window stays visible even if it started years earlier: the bar clips at the plot edge and gets a chevron. Never make a running medication disappear from a narrow window. The window start is pulled back off month-end overflow (31 Jul − 3 months is 30 Apr, not 1 May) so it can't be a day short.
- **Order matters:** apply the override first, then the window. Filtering on recorded dates first would hide a bar the doctor just moved into view.
- **Nothing vanishes quietly.** A ticked lab parameter with no in-window reading keeps a (short) lane saying so — a missing lane would read as "never measured". Entries whose stored date can't be read are dropped from the plot but counted, with an amber note saying so and that the record still holds them.
- Editing is owner-only (`canEdit` = not assistant mode **and** `patient.doctorId === activeWorkstationId ?? user.id`); the server enforces the same in `patients.controller.ts`. Every edit is written to the activity feed under `Health monitoring` — from the mutation's `onSuccess`, so a failed save is rolled back and announced rather than logged as if it landed. The patient id travels inside the mutation payload, not a closure: a blur fires as the lookup steals focus, which is exactly when `currentPatientId` is changing.
- **Bar durations: the mentions, and — since 07/11/2026 — the End of a course whose duration can be read.** A bar runs from the earliest mention of the exact drug name to the latest mention or, if later, the End the Drug history table shows (`lib/drugCourse.ts`: the start plus the days of the medicine's line and of the tapering lines under it, one after the other). The physician's sheet asked that a medicine added or edited in Patient's Prescriptions & reports go to Health monitoring "with its date", the bar from its Start to its End. A tapering `(cont)` line belongs to the medicine directly above it by position, as everywhere else in the app (before this it extended nothing). It still understates, or draws a point, where a duration cannot be read (no unit, `Continue`), where the entry has no real date (the no-date marker), and for a legacy `Current:` entry, whose date is only today's — don't "fix" those by guessing in `drugHistorySummary.ts`. **Two courses of one name are ONE bar** from the first start to the last end, the gap between them drawn as taken; one bar per course would change how the overrides (keyed by name) work and was not built. The chart's own duration override still wins. Pinned in `historySummaries.test.ts`.
- **A typo is not an instruction.** Every rejection is stated in place and nothing is stored: an unreadable date, a date more than 5 years out (the DDMMYY century trap), and an end that falls before the start. `buildTrack` still clamps an inverted range so nothing draws backwards, but the clamp is the drawing-time backstop, never the whole answer — clamping silently would hand the doctor a one-day bar with no reason for it.
- Keyboard: Enter commits, Escape abandons. Escape must set a ref **before** blurring — `blur()` runs the commit synchronously, ahead of React flushing the reverted draft, so without it Escape saves the value it was meant to discard.
- **⚕️ A number typed with a comma is not plotted as the part in front of the comma (`lib/numericInvSeries.ts`, 2026-10-12).** A value's parts are joined by commas, so `WBC:1,200` is stored looking exactly like a WBC of 1 followed by another part. Where the field has a unit the cut-off `1` has none and was already refused; where it has NONE — eight boxes in the catalog: Ascites Fluid › WBC (the one count), INR, A/G ratio, SAAG, TC/HDL, T-score, Z-score and METs — the 1 was plotted: a count of 1,200 drawn on the chart as 1 (in production until the release that carried this; found in the third review). **The rule (third form, and the first that looks at the thing itself):** the parts of a value are joined by a comma and nothing else — no space — so **a comma with a digit directly on each side of it cannot be read**, and what stands on either side of it is not plotted. `WBC:1,200` is 1,200, or 1.2 typed the continental way, or a 1 and another box's `200`. The one thing that says otherwise is another box's own name after the comma (`WBC:350,ADA:Positive`; OGTT's `Fasting:95,1 hr:160`). A comma followed by a SPACE is punctuation — `350, repeat next week`, `2.5, 3 days after dose change` — and the number before it stands, as it does in production (the fourth round's rule had stopped drawing those; the fifth put them back). The two earlier forms each let a wrong number through, in production: the first refused only a bare number followed by another bare number, so `1,200/cmm` was still drawn as 1 (fourth review); and both looked at the comma only from the number's side, so in the 108 boxes that are stored with no name in front of them — nearly every one-box test: ferritin, creatinine, HBV DNA — a ferritin typed `>2,000` (stored `>2,000ng/mL`) was drawn as **0**: the part after the comma, `000ng/mL`, was read as a value of its own, and `<0,5` as 5 (fifth review). `numericInvSeries.test.ts` now types ten such values into every chartable box of the catalog, as the popup stores them, and holds that none gives a point — and that the same number typed plainly does. **Two readings are lost on purpose:** ETT's METs is followed by a Report box that writes no name of its own, so `METs:10,150 bpm at peak` could be METs 10 and a report or a METs of 10,150 (as `10,5 METs` is 10.5): it is not plotted when the report begins with a number. And two bare numbers are not read however they are spaced (`1, 200`). **What the space rule still lets through, `DEFECT-HM-SPACED` (`it.fails`; sixth review; production draws the same):** a NUMBER typed with a comma and a space inside it is read as a piece of itself — `WBC:1, 200/cmm` as 1 in a box that writes its name and has no unit, and in a box stored with no name `> 2, 000` as 0, `approx 1, 200` as 200 and a decimal typed `,5` as 5. Telling `1, 200/cmm` from `2.5, 3 days after dose change` is reading what a comma means, and that is the physician's to say; a test beside the failing one writes down what is drawn today. **Still open, `DEFECT-HM-GROUPED` (`it.fails` in `numericInvSeries.test.ts`):** a number typed with its groups is not plotted — `WBC:12,000cells/uL` and `PLT:1,50,000/cmm` give no point, and the lane reads as if nothing was recorded. Reading `12,000` as twelve thousand is the other half of the fix, and what a typed comma means is the physician's to say (`FUNCTIONAL-AUDIT.md`). **Also never read: a number with a sign.** A DEXA T-score of `-2.5` gives no point — the parser has never taken a minus, and a test pins that a value beginning with one is dropped; T-score and Z-score are the two boxes in the catalog whose usual value is negative (`FUNCTIONAL-AUDIT.md`).
- Each reading carries its parsed `ms` from the memo that builds the series; the render path compares numbers. Typing in a duration box re-renders the whole chart, so don't move date parsing back into render.
- **⚕️ On a narrow screen the chart is not shrunk (2026-10-12).** It is one drawing, 800 units wide, and it used to be stretched or SHRUNK to its box: at 375px a lab value stood 5px tall. Below about 720px of box (`NARROW_BELOW`; measured with a `ResizeObserver`, in the page's px) every unit is 1.1px instead — a value 14px tall on screen — and the plot scrolls sideways, opening on the NEWEST readings, which are at the right-hand end and are read first. **The names stay put, and they are the same drawing seen through a second window** (`viewBox` on the name strip, another on the plot): a second, separately laid-out column of names could drift off the lane or the bar it names. **⚕️ A name that does not fit the strip takes TWO lines there, and for a medicine it is the dosage form that gives way** (`nameLines` in `lib/timelineLabel.ts`). The first version cut one line at its end, on the reasoning that the start of a name is what says which medicine it is — and in this app the start is the dosage form. The strip holds 13 to 15 characters on a phone (20 at most, on a narrow tablet), so `Tablet. Entaliv 0.5 mg` and `Tablet. Entaliv 1 mg` both read `Tablet. Enta…` and `Capsule (Enteric Coated). Sergel 40 mg` read `Capsule (Ente…`: two bars under one name, a dose change that could not be read off the timeline (caught in review, before release). **What the chart needs is that two names that are different are not drawn the same — and no rule for ONE name can promise that.** Two lines of thirteen letters do not hold every label, and whatever a rule leaves off, some pair differs in exactly that: with everything below in place, a tablet and an injection of one medicine and strength were both `…Ondansetron` over `8 mg`, two salts of calcium were both `Cal… + Vit.…`, two combinations beginning with amlodipine at the same strengths were one name, and four parameters of Hb electrophoresis were four lanes called `Hb / Electrophores…` — while this file said "never" (fourth review, before release). So it is done in two steps. `nameLines` lays ONE name out, keeping what most often tells two bars apart, the brand and the strength (the rest of this bullet). **`namesApart` lays out the names of one CHART together** — the bars as one list, the lanes as another — and any that came out the same are written again, in the first of four ways that leaves FEWER pairs alike on the whole chart than before. (1) Medicines that differ in their dosage form get it back, shortened, with the strength still under it (`Tab… Ondanse…` / `Inj… Ondanse…`, each over `8 mg`). (2) Where two forms BEGIN alike and are shortened to the same thing — `Tablet (Delayed Release).` and `Tablet (Extended Release).` are both `Tab…` beside the brand — the form takes a line of its own, cut at its end, with the start of the brand and the strength under it (`Tablet (Dela…` over `Pantoni…40 mg`; not the quiet line here: it is what tells the bars apart). **⚕️ Under a form that came back, a strength too long for its line is cut so that what it differs in from the OTHER strengths of that medicine on the chart stays in sight** (`strengthFor`): the two are compared part by part, and a part that differs is not the one left off — `Tabl… Edeloss` / `Inje… Edeloss`, each over `10 mg…12.5 mg` and over `10 mg…25 mg`; `Tablet. Napa` over `…500 mg` and over `…1000 mg`. Cut at its end whatever the chart held, a tablet and an injection of one strength stayed under one name — the form over the cut strength was the name the other strength already had — and with a third bar that was one of the strengths written another way (`Tab` for `Tablet.`), a dose change read `10 mg+160 mg+…` on every bar (seventh review, before release). Both ways are for names that DIFFER in their form: where every name has the same one, the room is the brand's. (3) The line that was cut is made to show BOTH its ends — its start, a mark, and the word the names part at: `…Exforge HCT` over `5 mg…12.5 mg` / `5 mg…25 mg`; `…Amlod…Atenolol` / `…Amlod…Losartan`, each over the whole `5 mg + 50 mg`; the lanes `Hb` over `Electroph…HbA` / `…HbA2` / `…HbF` / `…HbS`. The start is kept at the same length across the group, and it never ends inside an amount (`12500…` for 12500.5 is not shown; the start goes instead). (4) Failing that, the name is read from the place itself, under what the names share (`Burning sensat…` over `…2 weeks` / `…3 weeks`), with a mark in front of what is left wherever the line above was cut. **⚕️ And a strength longer than a line is cut by the other strengths of its medicine wherever it stands — not only under a form that came back (eighth review).** For one round `strengthFor` was reached only by names that had come out the SAME as another. Two labels that differed in something else — the form written `Tablet` with no full stop, which is read as the start of the brand, or another form altogether — were never written again, and each was cut as if it stood alone: `…Edeloss` over `10 mg+160 mg+…` beside `Tablet Edeloss` over `10 mg+160 mg+…`, for 12.5 and for 25; or the strength was left off whole for the sake of the brand (`…Amlodipine +` over `Olmesartan…`). So `namesApart` ends with a pass over the strengths of ONE medicine (`medicineOf`: the brand — with a short first word that is part of the name in front of it, and without a form word written with no full stop). A name that was not written again is laid out once more with the other strengths of its medicine beside it (`layOut`): where something of it has to be left off, the strength has the second line to ITSELF, cut by what it has to be told from, and the brand gives way above it — `Tablet. Edeloss` over `10 mg…12.5 mg`, `Tablet Edeloss` over `10 mg…25 mg`. The name keeps that unless it would put it under one name with another — **and only where it costs NOTHING of the medicine's name** (ninth and tenth reviews, of this change before it was released). The strength takes its line from the END of the brand, and the end of a brand may be all that says which medicine a bar is. With `Exforge` on the same chart, `Tablet Exforg…` stood over a strength with nothing left to say it was the HCT; a guard written for "what the two medicines share" then missed the shorter name of the two — the plain `Exforge`, beside the HCT — and a name behind a form the app cannot set aside read `Oral Solutio…`, no medicine at all. So the rule is the one that needs no judgement: **a name keeps the lines it has by itself unless the new ones show as much of its medicine's name, from its start, as those did.** A form word typed with no full stop (`Tablet Edeloss`) gives way where there is no room for it, so that the brand need not: `…Edeloss`, `…Exforge HCT`. (A name still drawn the same as another takes the line whatever it costs, as any way of telling two names apart does. And two ways of writing ONE medicine and strength — `Tablet.` picked, `Tablet` typed — are offered the very same lines: one of them has them, the first by its own name whatever the order of the list, and the other keeps its own; refused to both, neither bar showed the part that tells them from the other strengths.) Against the release before, over 493,920 charts of two medicines that begin alike: not one label of 1,481,760 shows less of its medicine's name, 99,957 show more, and two strengths of a medicine are told apart in 5,918 charts where they were not — never the reverse. **What the rule gives up:** a brand longer than a line (`Amlodipine + Olmesartan` written out) shows more of itself by itself than above a strength, so there a dose change is as unreadable as it was — `…Amlodipine +` over `Olmesartan…`, twice — in about one two-bar chart in a hundred of that brand (1,203 of 113,568; 1,683 in the release). A name that fits its two lines whole is left as it is; so is a medicine with no other strength on the chart, a label of the same medicine with NO strength being no other strength; and the same medicine typed in another case is the same medicine. (Not matched: a form of two words written with no full stop — `Oral Solution Avolac` — which still reads as part of the name; such a name is never cut for a strength.) **What is kept of a strength is what tells it from the MOST of the others** (`strengthFor`). It used to be whatever showed the most of the parts that differ, and a THIRD strength on the chart that differed in every part made every part count: the cut at the end, with two of three in sight, stood level with the first part and the last, and 12.5 and 25 read `10 mg+160 mg…` again (seen in a browser, on a made-up chart, before this was committed). Every later part that differs is tried too, with what follows it and alone — `…500 mg` beside `…1000 mg`, where a third strength has another first part. **This is counted for TWO strengths of a medicine on a chart, not for three:** gone through once by hand — every three of the fourteen strengths, under every three of six ways of writing a form that are not all the same (`Tablet.`, `Injection.`, `Tab`, `Capsule (Enteric Coated).`, `Tablet` with no stop, none), two brands, every width from 13 to 20: 1,223,040 charts — 158 still showed the same numbers for two of them, all in thirteen and fourteen letters (a `12.5` or a `25` that is the first part of one strength, `12.5 mg+…`, and the last of another, `…12.5 mg`). That count is not in the suite, and another pool gives another figure. A chart whose names are already different is left alone **but for this** (this file said "is not touched"). **⚕️ Why "fewer pairs alike", and why the marks:** the first version had two ways and took whichever applied, round after round. Of four strengths of one tablet it told one pair apart by showing their last part — which made them the same as the other pair — and the next round turned it back: two strengths under one name, and `12.5 mg` on a line by itself, unmarked, as if it were the whole strength of a 5 mg + 160 mg + 12.5 mg tablet (fifth review, before release). Now a step is taken only when it leaves fewer pairs alike on the whole chart — so the work ends, because every step lowers a count — and a part is never shown as if it were the whole. (That is all the count promises. A later step may write a name again, from the lines that name has by itself and not from the last step's, and may put two names together that an earlier step had told apart, so long as fewer pairs are alike after it than before: this file said "no step is ever undone" for one review round, and that was not true.) A name is written the same wherever it stands in the list. **What is held, and what is not** (`timelineLabel.test.ts`): TWO names that differ in more than their spacing are written differently — 20,000 pairs built to be mistaken for each other on every run (the same start and end, one word or one letter apart, or one going on after the other), a different 20,000 each time, **and a sample**: on 2026-10-13 a run failed, and drawn two million times the property left 27 pairs alike — in the code that had just been released, too. The rest are the last of the four known cases below, which the property leaves out by name. A full stop after a unit (`mg.25 ml`) was read as a decimal point once a mark stood before it; and a short piece of a line (`25`) was found first INSIDE the hidden number before it (`12125 mg`), so that what was brought into view stopped there, `121`. (One more was written down in the sixth review — two forms that begin alike, of one medicine in two strengths — and closed in the seventh by way (2).) Outside the shapes that are counted, a label may also show NO strength at all, where a long brand and a long strength both have to give way (`…Salmeterol +` over `Fluticasone…`) or where the strength is written in brackets: always possible with no other strength of its medicine on the chart, and still possible with one, where giving the strength the line would put two names under one or cost the medicine's name a letter. And what a cut is told from is the other strengths as they are WRITTEN, not as their labels show them: with three or more strengths of a medicine that differ in several places, two labels may each hide the part the other shows — `40 mg…12.5 mg` beside `40 mg+10 mg+…`, which both fit a 40 mg+10 mg+12.5 mg. And under a form on a line of its own, what follows the brand's mark may be only the LAST part of a strength — `Jardim…500 mg` for 12.5 mg+500 mg: the one mark stands for the rest of the brand and the start of the strength both. The bar's tooltip and the list under the chart carry every name in full. Now, for one name, the form stands above, smaller and paler, and the brand with its strength under it, whole; where brand + strength are too long for one line the brand takes the first line (cut at its END if it must be) and the STRENGTH keeps the second, whole — two strengths of `Amlodipine + Olmesartan` differ in their last few characters, which is what a cut at the end takes first; a symptom or a test's name wraps at a word and only its END is cut. **⚕️ A number is never shown in part, and never parted from its unit by a mark (sixth review, before release).** An AMOUNT is a number with the short word after it that says what it is a number of — `12.5 mg`, `500mg`, `10%`, `200 IU`, `30/70`, `12 weeks` — and a `…` never stands inside one: a cut backs off to where the amount begins, the word two names part at begins there too, and where a name has to be cut its line does not turn inside one either. Until then a cut fell wherever the line ran out: `12.5 mg + 500 mg` was `12.5 mg + 50…` (fifty, to the eye) and a 12.5 was `12.…`. And the word a strength parts at was looked for between SPACES — while **a combination picked from the medicines table has no space round its plus** (`5 mg+160 mg+12.5 mg`). That is known from the one label in the tests that came from a real sheet, `Edeloss 20 mg+50 mg`, not from reading the production table, which was not done for this; the rule holds for both ways of writing it. Every example this file had was spaced: of `10 mg+160 mg+25 mg` the chart showed `10…mg+25 mg`, which reads as a tablet of 10 mg + 25 mg — the unit beside the 10 was the hidden 160's. Now that pair reads `10 mg…25 mg` / `10 mg…12.5 mg`, and `12.5 mg+500 mg` beside `12.5 mg+1000 mg` reads `…500 mg` / `…1000 mg`. Five smaller things hold the same line: a number glued to a WORD is not cut either (`B12`, `Ciprofloxacin500mg`) and its unit is not left standing after the mark — though where two names are told apart it is part of its word (`HbA2` beside `HbA`, `FT3` beside `FT4`); a ratio may be cut at its stroke (`100 mcg/…`); a decimal point belongs to its number — `.5 mg` is never shown as `5 mg`, a part beginning `.5` is set apart from a mark before it by a space (`… .5 mg`, not four dots in a row), a word that begins with a point begins the strength like one that begins with a digit, and a full stop with a digit straight after it ends no dosage form (`Tab Rivotril .5 mg` had `Tab Rivotril .` read as its form and `5 mg` as its name) — where that point is the label's FIRST full stop, everything before the word is the name and the strength still has its line (`Tab Clonazepam .5 mg` is `Tab Clonazep…` over `.5 mg`; until the eighth review it was `Tab` over `Clonazepam…`, with no strength); a unit is not left standing where the cut has hidden its number; and a name may still turn between a number and its unit when that is the only way the rest of it fits (`…Exforge HCT 5` over `mg+160 mg+12.5 mg`) — nothing after the turn is left off, so the number and its unit are both there to be read. **What a unit is, is a guess that only ever hides:** the word of one to five letters after the number, whatever stands between them — so `30/70 as` holds together too: `Insulin Mixtard 30/70 as before` reads `Insulin` over `Mixtard…` in thirteen and fourteen letters, cut before its 30/70 and never inside it. (This file gave `Mixtard 30/70 as before` as the example; that name fits its two lines whole.) **The limit** (seventh review — the sixth's rule did not look for an amount at all once it was too long to keep whole, and `1,200,000 IU` was cut to `1…`): an amount is kept whole where a line can hold it between two marks — eleven characters in a line of thirteen. A longer one keeps its NUMBER whole and may lose its unit behind the mark (`10,000,000…`); a run of numbers longer than that is cut at one of its strokes (`150/75/400/…`); and only a single number longer than that is cut where it stands, as a long word is — no line could hold it. Each is pinned. Held for any chart by a property that finds the amounts with a letter-by-letter scan of its own, not the rule's pattern — 20,000 charts a run, more than half of them built from real-looking strengths, with and without a space round the plus. - **Only months INSIDE the plot get a line, and their names keep one rhythm** (`ticksInPlot` in `lib/timelineGeometry.ts`). `monthTicks` returns one tick per calendar month the range touches, and the first of those months starts before the first reading — to the left of the plot, where its line and its label ran through the column of names: "Mar 26" stood under a medicine's name as if it were that medicine's date. Three rules, each held for any range by a property test. **One rhythm:** the names are every month, or every 2nd, or every 3rd — never a mix. Thinned one at a time by the gap each happened to leave, a 16-month axis read `Jun · Aug · Sep … Feb · Apr`, because a month is 28 to 31 days wide; a time axis is read by its spacing before its words. **Inward, no further than the edge:** a name within half its width of an edge is written from the plot's edge (`lx`), as near its own line as the plot allows; if that runs it into its neighbour, the edge one gives way. **⚕️ The month already under way at the left edge is NAMED there, with no line**, when there is room before the next month's own line and name — its first day is not on the plot, and without this a five-week axis showed September's readings beside the one word `Oct`.
