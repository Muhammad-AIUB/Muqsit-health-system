# Domain — Prescribing: the visit, medicines, what the app learns, alerts

> Part of the shared vocabulary ([glossary](../DOMAIN.md)). Each entry is a concept with the physician's decisions behind it, moved here word for word from `docs/DOMAIN.md` on 2026-11-15. Keep it current in the same commit as the change (PRIME DIRECTIVE rule 7), and its line in the glossary with it.

## The visit and the prescription

- **Note and Plan (2026-09-07):** the OPD sidebar's single "Note / plan" list is now **two** lists, "Note" and "Plan", each edited in place like Final diagnosis and each printing as its own section. `Prescription.plan` is a new, additive column (`manual-prescription-plan.sql`); **`note` was not split up** — deciding which stored line was a plan is a clinical judgement, so every prescription written before this reads back unchanged. Both fields keep the ONE permission key the combined field had (`rx.note`) and declare it explicitly, because "Plan" is also the IPD sheet's label and IPD is deliberately outside the assistant keys. See `client/CLAUDE.md`.
- **Visit-date model:** the prescription header Date (`ptDate`) stamps investigation findings, on-examination entries, and drug history. Drug history splits Current vs Distant-past **by date**, automatically, on the next visit.

- **Incomplete prescription:** editor auto-saves per-doctor drafts; a patient with content but no "Save & print" carries `incompleteRx` and shows an Incomplete badge in OPD; printing completes it. A change made AFTER printing makes it incomplete again (19/10/2026) — parked, Incomplete in OPD, until it is printed again or put back as it was printed — and the printed mark survives a reload. The same prescription saved again within 30 minutes (a save whose answer was lost, pressed again) is the one on record, never a second row — and the doctor app sends such a save again by itself while its answer is lost, and says it MAY not have been saved only when no answer comes (20/10/2026); a patient added again to today's queue while still waiting, their prescription not yet printed, keeps their token (printed already, or marked Done, they are queued again). Since 2026-07-30 it is also **doctor-initiated**: the "Save to complete later" button parks an unfinished visit deliberately (`saveDraftNow`, see `client/CLAUDE.md`; labelled "Save draft" until 2026-08-15). Not permission-gated — it persists exactly what the background auto-save already writes for whoever is typing, so gating it would only lose their work.

- **The printed sheet's header, margins and signature (2026-10-07, physician's decisions).** In Prescription settings → Page Setup the two bands above and below the printable area are **Top margin** and **Bottom margin** (they read "Header Height" / "Footer Height", which said the header printed there). They are blank paper, for a pre-printed pad; the stored fields are still `headerHeight` / `footerHeight`. The **Header Section** the doctor designs (name, degrees, department — one box, or two side by side) now **prints inside the printable area**, at the top of every page, above the rule and the patient details, in the sizes set in the editor ("header a ja likhbo oita printable area a print hobe"). Until then that editor's output printed nowhere. The **signature line is fixed at the foot of the page**, beside the MHS mark, on every page, however short the prescription ("name sob somoi nicha fixed thakbe"); it used to hang under the last ℞ line. The **Footer Section** prints too (same day, "footer o print hbe"): under the signature row, the last thing on every page — chamber, hours, contact. Both HTML blocks are cleaned on the way to paper (`sanitizeHtml`, images allowed only from a web address or an upload).

## Medicines

- **"Your selected medicine names are:" (same sheet, item 6):** the Add Bulk Medicine box names what is ticked — what Add will add, in that order — above the medicines, so nothing ticked is forgotten.

- **Add Bulk Medicine (2026-10-13):** a **+** beside the ℞ mark of the OPD pad opens the medicines THIS doctor has prescribed before — never a combined list — each with a tick box; the ticked ones are added to the pad with their dose, when to take, duration and tapering lines already filled in. The physician's decisions: **one row per medicine**, with the instruction given to the most patients (another dose is changed on the pad, or reached by typing as before); two orders, **Popular** (most patients first, the order it opens in) and **Alphabetical**; and the list is **personal**. It is a faster way to what "Your usual" already offers one medicine at a time, so it inherits the habit rules rather than adding any: it reads the same `DoctorRxHabit` rows and nothing else (`GET /rx-habits/all`), the instruction shown for a medicine is the one the typing dropdown would offer first (pinned, then most distinct patients, then most recent), a dose the doctor hid is not offered, nothing is filled in that was not written, a taper stays with its medicine, scope is the workstation doctor, and an assistant needs the pad's own key. A line already on the pad is not added twice, and the prescribing alerts fire as if the medicines had been picked from the medicine list, because each one the catalogue lists carries its generic (a medicine it does not list has none, and its row in the box shows none). It adds no medicine the doctor has not prescribed, stores nothing and teaches nothing — only a saved prescription does. Two things follow from the habit rules and are the physician's to weigh (`FUNCTIONAL-AUDIT.md`): a medicine only ever saved with no dose, timing or duration is not in the list (an empty line is not a habit), and "most patients" counts the patients given the instruction shown, not everyone given that medicine at any dose. OPD pad only. See `client/CLAUDE.md`.
- **Monthly cost from the medicine chart (2026-10-14):** the "Total monthly approximate cost" box on the prescription header is priced from the `medicines` table (physician's decision: "take from medicine chart — but remember like Sergel 20 and Sergel 40 mg is not same"). A line's medicine is the chart's row by the pad's own comparison — form and strength included, so two strengths are two prices — and only what can be read whole is priced: a tablet, capsule or suppository the chart prices by the unit, a dose written as counts (`1+0+1`), a duration in days, weeks or months or Continue, a food cell that says only when beside a meal (one that may say how often — `once a week` — is not priced), over a 30-day month. A bottle, vial or tube is priced by the pack, and the cost of one dose of it is not guessed. Every medicine not priced is counted beside the total and named with its reason; a chart that could not be reached shows no total at all. Nothing is stored or printed. See `client/CLAUDE.md`.

## What the app learns from the doctor

- **Prescribing habit ("Your usual", 2026-08-17):** the ℞ pad learns a doctor's own repeated instructions and offers them back in the medicine dropdown. A **habit** is one row of `DoctorRxHabit`: `(doctor, medicine INCLUDING strength, whole instruction block)` — where a *block* is a head medicine plus its `>>>` tapering lines, so clicking one fills every line at once. Five rules carry the whole design:
  - **It never originates clinical content.** Every character offered is echoed verbatim from a prescription that doctor already saved and printed. Nothing is auto-filled — insertion requires a click — and the feature never writes to `Prescription`/`PrescriptionItem`. "Deleting" a suggestion sets `hidden`; there is no DELETE route.
  - **The key includes the strength, exactly.** `Tablet. Napa 500mg` and `Tablet. Napa 665mg` are different habits, always. Normalisation (`server/src/rx-habits/normalise.ts`) folds **typography only** — case, whitespace, an abbreviated dosage form (`tab.`→`tablet.`), the `500 mg`→`500mg` gap, and a trailing `n/a`. It must never drop a parenthesised form qualifier (`Tablet (Enteric Coated).` is not `Tablet.`, `SC Injection.` is not `Injection.`) and never converts a unit. Pinned in `normalise.spec.ts`.
  - **`patientCount` is DISTINCT PATIENTS, not prescriptions.** One patient returning monthly, a re-saved visit or a reprint must not make a one-off dose look routine — the count is the only thing separating routine practice from a special case, so it is established by asking the record, never by `increment: 1`. In production, Napa `1+1+1 · 5 days` appears on 7 prescriptions but only 2 patients.
  - **Only completed prescriptions teach.** Habits are written from `Prescription` rows, so drafts, `incompleteRx` and "Save to complete later" never count. An **empty block is not a habit** — a drug line saved with all of dose/food/duration blank is an unfinished line.
  - **Scope is the workstation doctor** (`@WorkstationDoctorId()`): an assistant sees the doctor they are assisting; a supervising doctor sees their own. No doctor ever sees another's. OPD only in v1 — the IPD order sheet, the Drug-history pad and the template editor render the same `MedicinePad` and deliberately do not opt in.

  The table is **derived and rebuildable**: `node server/scripts/rebuild-rx-habits.js [--dry-run]` recomputes it from the record. It re-applies `hidden`/`pinned` **by content, never by signature** — a signature is the output of the normalisation algorithm, so a signature join would resurrect every deliberately suppressed dose the day a rule is edited. **A Bangla dose / food / duration is the same instruction as its English** (physician's table, 2026-10-14; `normalise.ts#signatureOf` reads each cell through `rxLanguage.ts`, `ALGO_VERSION` 2): what a doctor writes in BAN and in EN is ONE habit with one distinct-patient count. When a rebuild folds two stored wordings into one habit, a `hidden` or `pinned` flag on **any** of them is kept — a dose the doctor suppressed does not come back because the same dose was also written the other way. See `server/CLAUDE.md` and `client/CLAUDE.md` for the write path and the UI.

- **Learned phrases ("Your usual notes", 2026-09-21):** the free-text sibling of the prescribing habit. The ADVICE list and the free-typed ℞ **note** lines (the italic lines between medicines, e.g. `Insulin as before`) are learned per doctor and offered back as they type — `DoctorPhraseHabit`, one row per `(doctor, source, phrase)` where `source` is `advice` or `rxNote`. **It inherits the five habit rules above verbatim**, because the failure modes are the same: it never originates clinical content (every character is echoed from a prescription that doctor saved and printed), `patientCount` is DISTINCT PATIENTS, only completed prescriptions teach, scope is the workstation doctor, and "deleting" sets `hidden` with no DELETE route. Normalisation folds **typography only** — case, whitespace and one trailing full stop — so `Insulin as before` and `Inj. Insulin as before` stay two different instructions, exactly as two strengths of one medicine do. Rebuildable with `node server/scripts/rebuild-doctor-phrases.js`. The per-device `User.fieldRecents` list is kept alongside it: a learned phrase can only come from a printed prescription, so recents are what covers a line typed earlier today. **Nothing the doctor wrote is cut or reset (physician's sheet, 31/10/2026):** the Advice popup offers every learned advice line (server bound 1000, `rxNote` keeps 8) and every recent line of any field (client bound 1000, was 12 — the 13th line used to push the oldest out of Note, Plan, Provisional diagnosis and Associated illness, which have no learned source); only a field's BUILT-IN list is held to ten places, and Advice has none any more. No patient count is written on a chip (it still orders them); a recent line is marked with a plus icon, a learned one with ★. Lines that had already left the old 12-slot recents list are not recoverable.

- **Special drug advice (℞ pad •••, 2026-09-24):** a doctor's OWN standing advice for a medicine, written in a box opened from the ••• beside the line's number. Two scopes: **Only for this medicine** (strength included, the same key as "Your usual") and **For all drugs of this group**, where group means **the same generic** (physician's decision — the medicines table has no drug class, and none is invented). A hand-typed medicine has no generic, so it cannot take group advice. Stored in `DoctorDrugAdvice` (`manual-drug-advice.sql`), scoped to the workstation doctor. While such a medicine is on today's pad, its **ticked** lines are copied into the prescription's **Advice** section and print as ordinary advice; they **leave the moment the medicine or its tick does**, and the next time the medicine (or any brand of its generic) is written they come back on their own, ticked. The tick is **per visit** (it rides the editor draft). The mirror **withdraws only lines it added itself** — a line the doctor typed into Advice by hand is never removed, even one that reads the same — and a mirrored line the doctor deletes from Advice by hand stays deleted for that visit. Nothing is invented or reworded; `Prescription.advice` remains the only record of what printed. OPD pad only. **Since 15/10/2026 (physician's sheet, item 4) a FINAL DIAGNOSIS has the same •••**, left of each line: advice saved for a diagnosis (scope `diagnosis`, matched by the diagnosis as written, case and spacing aside — `Pregnant 32 weeks` is not `Pregnant`) joins Advice the moment that diagnosis is written and leaves with it, by the same mirror and the same rules, and **the medicines' advice stands before the diagnoses'** in Advice and on paper. The app ships no diagnosis advice — every line is the doctor's. See `client/CLAUDE.md`.

## Alerts and safety

- **H/O drug hypersensitivity and High-Alert Medication (physician's sheet, 31/10/2026):** a section directly after Drug history on the OPD sidebar (desktop and phone), a per-PATIENT list kept for life (`PatientDrugSafety`, `/patients/:id/drug-safety`) and read by every practice that reaches the patient. One entry at a time with **+**: a medicine — picked from the medicines catalogue, whose row gives the generic, or used as typed with no generic — and one or both of "Is the patient hypersensitive to this drug?" / "Is it a high-alert medication?"; or a line written as it should read. Shown and printed as `Hypersensitive to NAPA(Paracetamol)` / `High-alert drug: NAPA(Paracetamol)` (brand in capitals up to its strength; brand alone when no generic is known) or the line as typed, marked in red. It has **no ⊘ Hide**: the printed sheet always carries it (clinical column, after Drug history's place; not on the privacy copy). **✎ Edit** changes ticks and words, and is the only place the ✕ appears; removal is soft (`removedAt`) with Undo. Who may change an entry: the owner's practice any, a supervising practice only its own; an assistant needs `rx.drugHistory`. It is NOT a prescribing alert: nothing on the ℞ pad reads it (`rxAlerts.ts` untouched) — linking it to the pad needs the physician's wording.

- **Prescribing alert ("MHS is suggesting"):** a live advisory shown at the top of "Notifications, Chats & Reports" while the doctor writes. Two rule kinds: **drug-condition** (a drug in the ℞ pad + a patient condition in a sidebar clinical field, e.g. entecavir + Pregnant) and **drug-drug** (e.g. sofosbuvir/velpatasvir + a PPI — the ℞ pad plus the patient's *current* drug history, with at least one of the pair on today's ℞). Rules live in `client/src/data/rxAlerts.ts` and every message there is **verbatim from a rule sheet supplied by the physician** — never reword one, never add a rule or a dose from clinical memory. Alerts are **derived, never persisted**: they recompute from editor state on every keystroke and vanish when the trigger is removed, so stored advice can never outlive the prescription that caused it. The banner shows **the advice and nothing else**: the `Because: <drug line> + <sidebar entry>` evidence line under it was removed on 2026-08-16 at the physician's request, since the advice already names the drug and the condition.

  **The ℞ pad shows the same advice behind ONE blinking red sign (2026-08-28).** It was a red speech bubble under the medicine from 2026-08-17; the physician's decision now is that the pad stays a writing surface — a single round sign lights up in the pad's toolbar the moment any warned medicine is written, however many rules fired, and each warning sits **in the flow, directly under its own medicine line** (`RxPadAlerts`) — a floating panel was tried the same day and dropped, because it moved around the screen whenever the pad or the page scrolled. **2026-09-24 (physician's flow):** a warning appears on its own when its medicine is written; "Ignore Warning" dismisses the message only — the sign stays lit as long as the medicine is on the prescription, pressing it shows the warning again, and it goes out only when the medicine is removed. One matcher still feeds both surfaces (`rxAlertsByLine`), because two versions of a contraindication that could disagree is worse than one behind a click — and nothing is actually hidden: the "MHS is suggesting" banner still lists every alert in full, and every alert is still written to the activity feed on save, ignored or not.

  **The warning is shown while writing and never printed or saved.** It was briefly rendered as a red callout on the printed sheet on 2026-08-17; the physician's decision the same day is that it must not survive onto the document — the printed prescription and the gallery snapshot show exactly what the doctor entered, not what the system inferred. `prescriptionDoc` has no `alerts` input at all now, and `prescriptionDoc.test.ts` pins the absence of the callout markup, its stylesheet and its red, so it cannot come back as a tidy-up. Restoring it is a product decision, not a bug fix. See `client/CLAUDE.md` for the matching rules and the known gaps.
