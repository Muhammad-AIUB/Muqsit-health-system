## Investigations and health monitoring

- **Disease / surgical history as dated lists (same sheet, item 3):** in On examination each takes several entries — the text, then a date typed `151026` → `15/10/2026` (four-digit year, the physician's choice in chat; only a date that has happened) — listed one under the other, each its own On-examination line (`Disease Hx: H/O cholecystectomy 15/10/2026`). Stored as before, one string per field, now one entry per line, so an old single value reads as one entry.
- **Disease / surgical history moved to the sidebar's History row (physician's sheet 28/10/2026, item 7 — "On examination er aituk prescription er history te nia jao purota, same format a"; built 02/11/2026, after the release before read the item as the "by Dr." card):** the two lists — a text box, a Date box (`151026` → `15/10/2026`) and Add, the entries under them — are under History in the prescription's left column, below this visit's own History lines, which are unchanged. They belong to the PATIENT and stay for every visit ("porer bar o … sob somoi thakbe"), written to the server at Add (no Save & print needed); every doctor who reaches the patient sees them and may add (owner's practice, assistants with History's key `rx.history`, a supervising doctor) — unlike My Personal Note, private to its writer. Stored as before, as `Disease Hx: …` / `Surgical Hx: …` entries of the shared On-examination history, so everything recorded through the popup since 18/10/2026 shows here unchanged and the records page keeps its section. × asks, takes the entry off SOFTLY (kept, stamped who and when) and offers Undo; the entry then shows nowhere (sidebar, records page, its On-examination card). **⊘ Hide** on the History row keeps the WHOLE History section off the printed sheet for that visit (the visit's lines and the patient's history) — OFF by default, because History always printed. Printed under History as `Disease Hx: …` / `Surgical Hx: …`, the words On examination printed before. The popup no longer has the boxes (one fact, one place); a visit parked before the move still shows and prints what it carried.
- **On examination is the PATIENT'S shared history (physician's sheets 28/10/2026 item 7, 31/10/2026 item 3):** a saved visit's On-examination lines are appended to `Patient.onExaminationSummary` by the server (`POST /patients/:id/on-examination`) — by the owner's practice AND a supervising one (a supervising doctor's findings used to be refused with the whole-list PATCH) — so every doctor who can reach the patient sees the same list, "like my personal note, but for all this patient's doctors". Each new entry carries `by`, the practice doctor's name, stamped by the server; an entry from before 31/10/2026 has none and shows none. On the records page each dated card reads `28/10/2026 by Dr. Shahariar`, and it is read-only there: the Edit (and its delete) is gone, a Download is in its place. **⊘ Hide** on the On-examination row takes the whole section off the printed sheet for that visit, like Drug history — but it starts OFF (the section prints), because it always printed. The records page also lists, after the Investigation summary: Drug history, Patient's input (nothing in the app is called that yet — an empty section and a question for the physician), Disease and surgical history (the On-examination popup's two history boxes, read back from the shared history, each entry once at the visit it was first recorded), Chief complaints history and Diagnosis history (each saved prescription of THIS practice, newest first — a supervising practice's prescriptions are not listed, server Rule 2).

- **Duration override (Health trend chart):** the chart's medication and symptom bars are *derived* — a medication spans first→last mention in `drugHistory`, a symptom spans first→last `Prescription.createdAt` carrying that complaint. Neither is the real-world duration (a tapering `(cont)` line carries no drug name, a legacy `Current:` entry has no real date), so the owner doctor can override a bar's start/end. Overrides live in `Patient.hmDrugDates` / `Patient.hmSymptomDates` (`{ [name]: { sf, upto } }`, blank side = keep the derived date) and are **display-only** — `drugHistory` and the prescriptions are never rewritten. Overridden bars render dashed and their tooltip shows the recorded range alongside the shown one; an override that resolves to the recorded range is dropped rather than stored, so "dashed" always means "moved away from the record". Owner-only, enforced server-side. See `client/CLAUDE.md` for the date-parsing traps this sits on.

- **Investigation groups (2026-09-24):** each doctor's own named sets of tests (`User.investigationGroups`), made and ticked in Advised tests / investigation → "Investigations group". Per signed-in user, never pre-filled by the system. See `client/CLAUDE.md`.

- **Choosing a date for findings is a deliberate press (2026-10-12).** In the Investigation popup the chosen day is the date every result typed below is stamped with, and changing it first files whatever is half typed under the OLD date. So the calendar's arrow keys only move along the days; a day is chosen by Enter, Space or a click — never by a key that only moves. (The date box, the month and year buttons, the month list, the year wheel and Today set the date too; each is a deliberate act, and each files the half-typed result under the old date first.) The arrows stop at the ends of the month — another month is its own button. Escape with the month list open closes the list, not the popup — wherever in the popup the cursor is.
- **The health-trend chart keeps its size (2026-10-12).** It is one drawing, and it is never shrunk to fit a narrow screen: at half size a lab value was 5px tall. On a phone or a tablet the plot scrolls sideways instead and opens on the newest readings, while the names of the tests and medicines stay in place beside it. There a medicine is named by its brand and strength with its dosage form on a small line above, and when even that is too long it is the name that is shortened before the strength is: cut at its end to fit, two strengths of one medicine (`Tablet. Entaliv 0.5 mg`, `Tablet. Entaliv 1 mg`) had the same name, and a dose change could not be read off the bars. Two short lines cannot hold every label, so the names of one chart are worked out together: where two that are different would come out the same — a tablet and an injection of one medicine and strength, two combinations that begin with the same ingredient, four parameters of one long-named test, several strengths of one tablet — they are written again so that the part they differ in is shown, with a mark (…) wherever something is left out, so that a part is never read as the whole. **A number in a name is never shown in part, and a unit never stands beside a number it does not belong to:** a strength too long for its line is cut between its parts (`5 mg+160 mg+…`), and where two strengths differ in a part that was cut off, that part is shown whole — `10 mg…25 mg` beside `10 mg…12.5 mg`. A tablet and an injection of one medicine keep their form as well. And what a long strength is cut to depends on the other strengths of that medicine on the chart, whether or not the two names would have come out the same: the strength has the second line to itself, and a part they differ in is what is kept — `10 mg…12.5 mg` beside `10 mg…25 mg`, also where one of the two was typed by hand as `Tablet Edeloss` and the other picked from the list as `Tablet. Edeloss`. (Until 2026-10-13 that was done only for names that would otherwise have been identical; two labels that differed in how the form was written both read `10 mg+160 mg+…`.) Two things bound it. The strength takes that line only where it costs nothing of the medicine's NAME: a brand that fits the line above keeps it whole (`…Exforge HCT` over `5 mg…12.5 mg`, also where "Tablet" was typed with no full stop), and a name that would show less of its medicine that way keeps the lines it has by itself — the end of a name may be all that says which medicine it is (`Exforge HCT` beside `Exforge`). So for a brand too long for a line, written out as two ingredients, a dose change may still not be readable from the names. And each label keeps what tells its strength from the others as they are written, not as their labels show them — with three or more strengths of one medicine that differ in several places, two labels may each hide the part the other shows. (A number too long for any line — twelve characters and more on a phone — is the one thing still cut where it stands.) (A combination picked from the medicines list has no space round its plus, `10 mg+160 mg+25 mg` — so the one such label seen on a real sheet is written — and until this was looked at such a strength could be shown as `10…mg+25 mg`, which reads as 10 mg + 25 mg; caught in review, before release.) Two short lines have a limit: names that differ in more places than they can show are still written differently where that is possible, but a name may then not say every difference, and in four known cases two bars are still left under one name — eight strengths of one tablet on one chart; a tablet and an injection of two medicines whose long names differ only at their end; the same where the strength is written in brackets; and two names that differ only in the unit of a very long number — on a phone, and not only the narrowest (`FUNCTIONAL-AUDIT.md`); the full name is on the bar's tooltip and in the list under the chart. A month's line is drawn only where it falls inside the plot — before, the first month's label stood to the left of the axis, under a medicine's name — the months are named in one even rhythm, and the month already under way where the plot begins is named at its left edge.

- **A number is shown as it was written (2026-10-12).** A result's parts are stored joined by commas, and the Investigation popup's "Added results" panel sets them apart with a space. It used to do that inside a number too: `ANC 1,500 cells/µL` was shown there as `ANC 1, 500 cells/µL`, and a platelet count typed `1,50,000` as `1, 50, 000`. A calculator's result, the result of a test with one box, and free text are now shown exactly as stored. A test with several boxes is shown as before — there the comma between two boxes must never be lost (ETT's METs `10` and a report beginning `150 bpm` must not read `10,150`), so a number typed with its groups into such a test is still set apart at them in that panel. The record itself, the sidebar and the printed sheet were always right.
- **A typed comma and the health-trend chart (2026-10-12, part open).** The chart reads the same stored value, where the commas between a test's boxes and the commas a doctor types inside a number look alike. Three wrong numbers came of it, each drawn in production until the release that carried this: a count typed `1,200` into a box with no unit was drawn as 1; so was `1,200/cmm`; and a ferritin typed `>2,000` was drawn as 0. The rule now: a comma with a digit directly on each side of it cannot be read, so nothing on either side of it is plotted — a number that cannot be read with confidence is not drawn. A comma followed by a space ("350, repeat next week") is punctuation, and the number before it is drawn as before — which leaves a NUMBER typed with a comma and a space inside it (`1, 200/cmm`) drawn as its first part, as it is in production today. Still open, and the physician's to decide: what such a spaced comma means; and a number typed with its groups (`12,000`, `1,50,000`) is not plotted at all, so its lane reads as if nothing had been recorded. Whether a typed comma is always a grouping comma here is a clinical reading, not a formatting one.
- **A result box with two units converts only a plain number (2026-10-12).** Forty boxes of the Investigation popup take their value in either of two units; typing in one fills in the other, and the doctor's preferred unit is the one recorded. Until the release that carried this the conversion read only the START of what was typed: a viral load typed `1,20,000` copies/mL was recorded, and printed, as `0.18IU/mL`; a creatinine typed `1,2` mg/dL as `88.42umol/L`, the conversion of 1 — a plausible number, and wrong; and a value that did not begin with a number at all (`>2000`, `Not detected`), typed in the box of the unit that was not the preferred one, was not recorded. Now only a whole, plain number is converted; anything else — a number with a comma, a power of ten, a remark — is recorded exactly as typed, in the unit of the box it was typed in. A value recorded wrongly before that release cannot be told from a real one in the record, and one that was not recorded is simply not there.
